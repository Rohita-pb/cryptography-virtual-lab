Double DES & Triple DES

Interactive demonstration of Double DES and Triple DES encryption and decryption using different key configurations.

01

Aim

To demonstrate the encryption and decryption of a message using Double DES and Triple DES with one, two and three keys.

02

Objectives

  • Understand the working of DES as a symmetric-key algorithm.
  • Understand the concept of applying DES multiple times.
  • Perform Double DES encryption and decryption.
  • Perform Triple DES using one, two and three keys.
  • Observe the individual stages of encryption and decryption.
  • Verify that decryption recovers the original message.
03

Theory

Data Encryption Standard (DES)

DES is a symmetric-key block cipher in which the same secret key is used for encryption and decryption. DES processes data in 64-bit blocks and uses a 64-bit key input, of which 56 bits are effective key bits and 8 bits are used for parity.

DES uses a Feistel structure consisting of 16 rounds. Although DES is historically important, its effective 56-bit key size makes it unsuitable for protecting modern sensitive information.

DES:
Plaintext → DES(K) → Ciphertext
Ciphertext → DES-1(K) → Plaintext

Double DES

Double DES applies the DES encryption operation twice using two independent keys, K1 and K2.

Double DES Encryption:
C = EK2(EK1(M))
Double DES Decryption:
M = DK1(DK2(C))

Triple DES

Triple DES applies DES three times using an Encrypt-Decrypt-Encrypt (EDE) structure.

Procedure

Double DES Procedure

  1. Enter the plaintext message.
  2. Enter Key 1 and Key 2 in hexadecimal format.
  3. Click the Encrypt button.
  4. Observe the first DES encryption using Key 1.
  5. Observe the second DES encryption using Key 2.
  6. Note the final ciphertext.
  7. Click Decrypt.
  8. Observe the reverse operations and verify that the original message is recovered.

Triple DES Procedure

  1. Enter the plaintext message.
  2. Select 1-key, 2-key or 3-key mode.
  3. Enter the required DES keys.
  4. Click Encrypt.
  5. Observe the three DES stages using the EDE structure.
  6. Note the generated ciphertext.
  7. Click Decrypt.
  8. Verify that the original plaintext is recovered.

⚠ Security Note

DES and Triple DES are legacy cryptographic algorithms. They are demonstrated here for educational purposes. Modern applications should use stronger algorithms such as AES rather than DES or Triple DES.
